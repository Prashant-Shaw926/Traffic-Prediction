Stage 2 dynamic data-driven junction relationship discovery
Generated from the Stage 2 and Stage 1 test run. These are lagged statistical associations, not road links and not causal effects.

No files under models/, backend/services/, or src/ were modified.

3. Relationship formula
C(source -> target, k) = Pearson correlation of V_source(t-k) and V_target(t).
Pairs are matched by exact timestamp. A missing hour is skipped. Adjacent rows are not treated as adjacent hours.

4. Candidate lags
(1, 2, 3, 24, 168)
Lag 168 stays configured. Inside the 168-hour window it produced 0 aligned pairs at both tested timestamps, because t-168 falls before the window start. Those lags were ignored. They were not filled and were not stored as zero.

5. Historical window definition
Closed interval [T - 167 hours, T], built by Stage 1 build_simulation_input from data/raw/traffic.csv.
2017-06-15 10:00 uses 2017-06-08 11:00 through 2017-06-15 10:00.
2017-06-25 10:00 uses 2017-06-18 11:00 through 2017-06-25 10:00.

6. Relationship selection rule
For each ordered pair with source != target, keep the candidate lag with the largest absolute correlation, provided it has at least 24 paired observations and a finite Pearson correlation. Ties keep the earlier lag in (1, 2, 3, 24, 168). The signed correlation is stored. There is no correlation threshold. Zero-variance series return no correlation for that lag.

7. Leakage prevention
Only Stage 1 observations at or before T are available. Pairing additionally rejects a timestamp after the window end. No future row is used.

8. Number of ordered junction pairs
4 x 3 = 12. Both tested timestamps returned all 12 pairs.

10. Whether relationships differ between tested timestamps
Yes. The two relationship tuples are not equal. All 12 ordered pairs differ in correlation, selected lag, or both. Examples: 1->3 changes from lag 24 (r=0.634535) on 15 June to lag 1 (r=0.591766) on 25 June. 3->4 changes from lag 24 (r=0.452990) to lag 1 (r=0.660246).

13. Limitations
- No selected relationship in either tested window is negative. A separate Pearson check on [1, 2, 3] against [3, 2, 1] stays negative, so a negative result would be kept. The dataset windows above did not produce one.
- Lag 168 cannot be estimated from a 168-hour window that ends at T, because the source hour is before the window.
- No strength threshold or network pruning is applied.
- These correlations do not identify physical roads and do not prove that one junction causes traffic at another.
